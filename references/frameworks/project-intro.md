# Framework — Project / product introduction (12-point)

For a deck that introduces a project, product or initiative and asks for
buy-in, funding or a green light. Developed from running IT and process
projects at scale and from creative direction — a business case that
still has to convince a room, not fill a form.

Rule carried from that practice: **avoid the "just an idea" stage. The
world has enough ideas and lacks the resources to make them work.** If
the deck cannot answer 7, 8 and 9 below with something real, it is not
ready to present.

## The twelve points

1. **Problem statement + business case.** What is not working, and what
   makes you say so. Why it matters to the business, what it costs to
   leave it, why now. State the project in three lines: why you do it /
   how you do it / what you do.
2. **Before and after.** How things work now versus how they work with
   the solution. A concrete pair, not an abstraction.
3. **Who it is for.** The clients or users, and specifically how their
   day gets easier or better.
4. **Goal and CTQs.** What success is. The critical-to-quality measures —
   the few things that must be true for this to have worked.
5. **In scope / out of scope.** What this project does. What it
   deliberately does not take on.
6. **Competitors / alternatives.** Who else solves this, including "do
   nothing", and what makes this approach different — one point of
   difference, not five.
7. **What is done so far.** Progress and findings to date: customer
   discovery, metrics, prototypes, conclusions drawn. Evidence it is
   past the idea stage.
8. **Team.** Who makes this real, and why they will succeed. Named
   people, relevant track record.
9. **Resources.** What you already have; what you still need.
10. **Risks and mitigations.** The delivery and operational risks, each
    with what you will do about it.
11. **Market potential.** Current value of the niche, and the share you
    aim to take. Order of magnitude, sourced.
12. **Business model.** How the budget works. How it is priced and sold.
    Revenue in plain terms.

## Deck sections

| Section | Points | Slides |
|---|---|---|
| The problem | 1 | problem as assertion headline; cost of inaction (data); why now |
| The shift | 2, 3 | before → after (one slide, split by a rule); who benefits and how |
| What we're aiming at | 4, 5 | goal + CTQs; in scope / out of scope (one slide, two columns) |
| Why us, why this | 6, 7, 8 | point of difference vs alternatives; traction to date (data); team |
| What it takes | 9, 10, 11, 12 | resources have/need; risks + mitigations; market size; model |
| The ask | — | the specific decision, amount, owner, date — CTA slide |

```
## Model

## Ask

```

If points 7–9 are thin, stop and tell the user the deck will not land —
the room will read it as an idea without a plan.
